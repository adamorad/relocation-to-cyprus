## 2. Choice and rationale

Chosen: (A) Cyprus digital nomad visa, (B) moving to Cyprus from the UK after Brexit.

Why A: highest impressions in the set (295), one page clearly owns it, and the intent (requirements, documents, process) is exactly what an information site is for. Position 47 with a well-structured but FAQ-less page suggests a quality/coverage gap rather than an authority wall. Ranking potential is realistic if the page becomes the most useful and most careful explainer.

Why B: broad "moving to cyprus" queries at pos 50 to 78 are a mix; the UK variant is the one with a clear matching page and a specific audience (post-Brexit third-country nationals) that generic relocation sites cover poorly. The existing page had a muddled entry-rules sentence and no decision or sequencing content, which is what "moving to" searchers want.

Skipped:
- Company formation / incorporation (pos 70 to 85): SERP dominated by law firms and corporate service providers; intent is commercial lead-gen. Existing three pages already cover it. Low realistic return.
- Property lawyers (pos 25 to 40): best position of the set, but the query is lead-gen (people hiring a lawyer). Guide and directory already exist; the directory is the right conversion asset. Worth revisiting for a title/meta tweak only.
- Cyprus IP box: narrow professional audience, already covered, low volume.
- Tax for expats / calculator: covered and already has embedded calculators; high competition from big tax and expat sites. Candidate for a later round.
- Retiring in Cyprus and best place to live: covered, decent fit, but lower impression evidence in the brief. Good round-two candidates.

## 4. Owner fact-check list (verify against official sources before publishing)

Official sources to use: Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD, moi.gov.cy/crmd), Cyprus Tax Department (mof.gov.cy/tax), Ministry of Interior, GESY/HIO (gesy.org.cy), GOV.UK and HMRC for UK items.

Important: the claims below are already live in the existing guide text (they were not introduced by this branch). I did not change them because I cannot verify them, but they need checking. Items marked NEW were introduced or edited by this branch.

### 4a. Digital nomad guide (`digital-nomad-visa-guide`)

1. Minimum income EUR 3,500 per month, stated as NET after deductions here. INCONSISTENCY: the UK guide says "gross". Confirm which, and whether it is per month or an annual equivalent.
2. Spouse uplift of 20% (to EUR 4,200) and 15% per dependent child. Confirm both percentages and whether the base for the child uplift is right.
3. Health insurance minimum EUR 30,000 and the claim that GESY registration does not satisfy it.
4. Required documents list: 12 months passport validity remaining, two photos, criminal record certificate, proof of accommodation, cover letter, three months payslips and bank statements (employees) vs three to six months (freelancers).
5. Application fee "approximately EUR 70 per adult as of 2025", payable at CRMD or via portal. Also: is there a separate fee for the ARC, and for dependants?
6. Portal name and path ("Visitor/Temporary Residence, Digital Nomad Visa" at crmd.moi.gov.cy) and whether application from inside Cyprus on tourist entry is currently allowed.
7. CRMD office list in the text (includes Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, Famagusta). Verify current offices. Nicosia is mentioned only as a factual CRMD office in existing text; the site policy is about maps, filters and nav.
8. Processing time: official target 5 to 8 weeks; realistic 6 to 10; peak 12+. ARC issued 2 to 4 weeks after biometrics.
9. Term: initial 1 year, renewable up to 2 further years, 3 years maximum; renewal to be submitted at least 2 months before expiry; behaviour after the maximum (re-apply after a qualifying absence).
10. Dependants: unmarried children up to 18, or up to 25 in full-time education; no right to work; access to GESY.
11. Permit cap: the guide has no statement on a cap on the number of permits. Confirm whether one exists, and if so, current numbers. (NEW guide text only tells readers to check this.)
12. Tax statements in the DNV guide: 60-day rule conditions (60 days in Cyprus, not resident elsewhere more than 183 days, not resident in another single country); non-dom 17 of 20 years test; SDC 0% on foreign dividends and interest for 17 years; SDC on rental income abolished from 1 January 2026; no general capital gains tax with the real-property exception (property in Cyprus, and shares where more than 50% of value is Cyprus real estate); income tax bands (0% to EUR 22,000, then 20%, 25%, 30%, 35% above EUR 72,000); GESY contribution 2.65% employees and 4% self-employed. Note the actual 60-day rule also requires carrying on a business or employment in Cyprus and holding a Cyprus residence, and not being resident elsewhere; the existing text omits these. Check against the Tax Department. Also note the 2026 tax reform may have changed bands and SDC.
13. NEW text: "the 183-day rule, or the 60-day rule with its own conditions" and "day-count tests applied to the calendar year". Generic, but confirm wording.

### 4b. UK guide (`moving-to-cyprus-from-uk`)

1. Visa-free entry for UK nationals "currently up to 90 days" (NEW wording, figure was already present). Confirm the limit and whether any ETA, ETIAS-equivalent or Cyprus entry pass is required now. Confirm that the 90 days is a Cyprus-specific allowance not tied to the Schengen 90/180 count.
2. Category F thresholds: EUR 2,500 per month individual, EUR 3,500 couple; one-year validity; renewals; permanent residency after five years. Also verify whether Category F is currently the correct name and whether it is still open to new applicants.
3. DNV threshold stated as EUR 3,500 gross here vs net in the DNV guide (same as 4a.1).
4. Permanent Residency by Investment: EUR 300,000 plus VAT for a new property from a qualifying developer; no right to work; family inclusion.
5. Work permit processing 4 to 8 weeks.
6. Tax treaty: 1974 UK-Cyprus DTA and "updated subsequently"; treatment of UK government service pensions (taxable in the UK) vs private pensions (taxable in Cyprus); 5% flat rate on foreign pension income above EUR 3,420 per year; dividend, rental and capital gains allocation.
7. Property: transfer fees 3% on first EUR 85,000 and 5% above (and the fee bands above that, plus the 50% reduction for resales); stamp duty abolished 1 January 2026; Cypriot bank mortgage terms for non-residents (30 to 40% deposit, 3.5 to 5% rates); claim that Council of Ministers approval is no longer a practical barrier for non-EU buyers.
8. Driving licence: exchange at Limassol, Larnaca or Paphos; medical certificate cost EUR 30 to 50; exchange fee EUR 35 to 55; 2 to 6 weeks; no test required.
9. Healthcare: GHIC validity in Cyprus and the description of what it covers; GESY contribution 2.65%; GESY registration requirements (TIN, address, permit); dental not covered.
10. NEW text: HMRC "P85 route" for notifying departure, and the Statutory Residence Test deciding UK tax residency. I am confident both exist but confirm current process on GOV.UK. Also confirm the claim in the existing FAQ that you get a certificate of Cyprus tax residency to present to HMRC.
11. NEW text: route-to-situation mapping (retirees to Category F, remote workers to DNV, job offer to work permit, property buyers to PR by investment). Confirm no situation has a better-fit route, and that "Category F" remains valid terminology.
